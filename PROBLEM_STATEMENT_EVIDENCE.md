## 2. Forecasts arrive, impact intelligence does not

### Chennai, December 2015. The reservoir release nobody was warned about.

- The CAG performance audit called the flood a man-made disaster. On 1 December 2015 the discharge from Chembarambakkam was raised abruptly from 12,000 to 20,960 cusecs, then held at 29,000 cusecs for 21 hours into the Adyar. The audit called this "a serious failure in operation of the reservoir".
- The release was made without public warning. The audit found no real-time flood forecasting or communication facility at the reservoir, operating rules not revised in 23 years, and no record of communication between the tank officer and the government.
- Reservoirs had filled in November and no precautionary release was made.
- The Tamil Nadu State Disaster Management Authority had not met once since its formation in 2013.
- Deaths: the state put the October to December toll at 470. Other reports give 289 to over 500. Economic loss was estimated at about USD 2.2 billion by an international study, and over Rs 15,000 crore by domestic sources.

**Slide line:** In 2015 Chennai had the rainfall warning. What it did not have was a forecast of what a 29,000 cusec release would do downstream, or a way to tell people before the water arrived.

Sources: [The News Minute on CAG](https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/tamil-nadu/cag-report-slams-chennai-administration-2015-floods-says-govt-was-unprepared-84476), [The News Minute on Chembarambakkam](https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/tamil-nadu/how-grave-lapses-chembarambakkam-lake-caused-chennai-s-manmade-flood-2015-84524), [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/news/india/cag-report-slams-tamil-nadu-govt-over-chennai-floods), [CAG Report 4 of 2017 executive summary](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2016/Executive_summary_of_Report_No_4_of_2017_-_Performance_Audit_of_Flood_Management_and_Response_in_Chennai_.pdf)

### Thoothukudi and Tirunelveli, December 2023. The forecast was off by a factor of four.

- Kayalpattinam recorded 95 to 96 cm of rain in 24 hours on 17 to 18 December, more than the town's normal annual total. IMD had forecast around 20 cm for the area.
- The red alert was issued on 18 December, after rain had started on the 17th. Thoothukudi and Tirunelveli were flooded by that afternoon.
- The Chief Minister said the state was not sufficiently forewarned. The Earth Sciences Secretary replied that no model had anticipated 90 cm. Both statements are true, which is the point: a point forecast with no uncertainty band and no impact translation leaves no room to act.
- The Chief Secretary reported 750 tank breaches across the districts plus a breach at Korampallam tank. Deaths: 22 in Thoothukudi and 16 in Tirunelveli by the Chief Secretary's count, 31 across four districts in another. Over 10,000 people were evacuated by boat and helicopter. About 250 villages were without power for five days.

**Slide line:** In December 2023 the forecast for Kayalpattinam was 20 cm. It got 95. Seven hundred and fifty tanks breached and nobody downstream had a map of which ones.

Sources: [The South First on 95 cm](https://thesouthfirst.com/tamilnadu/tamil-nadu-rains-thoothukudi-district-receives-95-cm-of-rainfall/), [The South First on Stalin's statement](https://thesouthfirst.com/news/rains-claim-10-lives-wasnt-sufficiently-forewarned-by-imd-says-stalin/), [Deccan Herald on IMD prediction](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/tamil-nadu/imd-prediction-could-have-been-more-accurate-tamil-nadu-govt-2817444), [DT Next on tank breaches](https://www.dtnext.in/amp/story/news/tamilnadu/flood-damage-assessment-to-be-over-in-next-3-days-says-chief-secretary-756541), [The Wire](https://thewire.in/environment/southern-tamil-nadu-rains-evacuations)

### Chennai, Cyclone Michaung, December 2023. Same wards as 2015.

- Over 40 cm fell in 36 hours across Chennai and three neighbouring districts. Meenambakkam recorded 250 mm and Nungambakkam 230 mm in 24 hours.
- Fourteen subways were closed by inundation. Eleven express trains were cancelled when water reached the danger mark at a rail bridge. The airport shut for over 13 hours and about 70 flights were cancelled.
- Velachery was among the worst hit, exactly as in 2015. A week later Kodungaiyur, Pallikaranai, Thoraipakkam and Valasaravakkam were still waterlogged, and residents blamed drains and missing pre-monsoon work.
- Deaths were reported between 5 in Greater Chennai police figures and 17 confirmed by police in wider counts.

**Slide line:** Eight years after 2015, the same neighbourhoods flooded again. The problem is not that the rain was unknown, it is that nobody could say which street would be under water and for how long.

Sources: [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Ftamil-nadu%2Fcyclone-michaung-leaves-trail-of-destruction-in-tamil-nadu-2797169), [Sphere India SitRep](https://www.sphereindia.org.in/sites/default/files/2023-12/SitRep-1_CycloneMichaung_04-12-23.pdf), [Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/devastation-storm-chennai-cyclone-michaung-monsoon-flooding-36477), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/cyclone-michaung-chennai-india-landfall-andhra-pradesh-deaths-evacuations-today), [DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/chennai/chennai-floods-water-yet-to-recede-in-many-areas-of-the-city-753596)

## 3. Drainage and encroachment: the city is built to flood

- The CAG audit found the rainfall intensity Greater Chennai Corporation used to design its storm water drains was wrong, producing lower-capacity drains that contributed to the 2015 flood. It also cited poor coverage, improper design and missing links in the network.
- A GCC chief engineer said in 2023 that most of the city's roughly 2,950 km of drains handle only 2 to 5 cm per hour. Upgraded drains target 8 cm per hour. The city needs at least 2,000 km more, by one of his figures 5,000 km.
- The audit found 22.7 km, or 28 percent, of the 80 km Cooum riverbank encroached by over 14,000 families and others, reducing the river's carrying capacity. It faulted the Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority for permitting encroachment on water bodies.
- Pallikaranai marsh: from about 5,000 to 6,000 hectares in the 1970s to under 600 hectares today, roughly 90 percent lost. Water-holding capacity fell from about 56 million cubic metres in 1975 to 5 million in 2018. The Greater Chennai Corporation dump yard alone occupies 174 hectares of it.
- Chennai lost 62 percent of its wetlands between 1980 and 2010 by Care Earth Trust's assessment. A Uvakai Research Foundation study found 165 water bodies, 31 lakes and 134 tanks, have disappeared from the Pallikaranai catchment, about 7.35 square kilometres of water across Velachery, Sembakkam, Nanganallur and nearby areas.
- Desilting before the 2014 and 2015 monsoons did not start on time because funds were released late.

**Slide line:** Chennai's drains were designed for 2 to 5 cm an hour. Michaung delivered 25 cm in a day. The marsh that used to absorb the difference is 90 percent gone.

Sources: [CAG Chapter 4 Encroachments](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2016/Chapter_4_Encroachments_of_Report_No_4_of_2017_-_Performance_Audit_of_Flood_Management_and_Response_in_Ch.pdf), [The South First on drain capacity](https://thesouthfirst.com/tamilnadu/chennai-needs-additional-higher-capacity-drains-to-handle-heavy-rains-official/), [DT Next on Pallikaranai](https://www.dtnext.in/edit/2022/06/06/from-50-kms-to-less-than-5-kms-how-pallikaranai-marsh-shrank-in-50-yrs), [SPA Bhopal study](https://dspace.spab.ac.in/handle/123456789/847), [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/climate-change/chennai-rain-floods-climate-change-crisis), [Madras Musings](https://madrasmusings.com/vol-35-no-3/restoring-pallikaranai-marshland-a-critical-imperative-for-chennais-ecological-and-urban-resilience/)

## 4. Commuters: people drive into water that navigation apps call a road

- **Bengaluru, KR Circle underpass, May 2023.** A 22-year-old Infosys employee drowned when her family's car went under in neck-deep water. The barricade had been knocked over by the rain. The Lokayukta called the civic body's failure to maintain drains maladministration.
- **Faridabad, September 2024.** Two bank employees drowned when their SUV sank in 10 to 12 feet of water in a railway underpass. Doors locked automatically as the car submerged.
- **Delhi, June 2024.** Three people, including two boys, drowned in waterlogged underpass areas in separate incidents.
- **Salem, Tamil Nadu.** An elderly couple died when their car sank in a flooded railway underpass near Omalur and the doors would not open.
- **Madurai.** Three occupants of an SUV were pulled from a submerged subway on Raja Mill Road by local youths with ropes.
- **Chennai, Michaung.** Fourteen subways closed. Ambulances, buses and delivery fleets had no flood-aware routing.
- **Bengaluru, 30 August 2022.** The Outer Ring Road Companies Association put the loss from one day of flooding at Rs 225 crore, mostly because staff were stranded on the road for over five hours. The corridor employs half a million to a million people and generates about USD 22 billion a year.

**Slide line:** Every monsoon, people drown in cars in underpasses that Google Maps still shows as the fastest route. One flooded afternoon on Bengaluru's Outer Ring Road cost Rs 225 crore.

## 5. Farmers: the same rain is a crop or a loss depending on the week

- **Cyclone Fengal, December 2024.** Villupuram received 42 cm on 1 December. The Chief Minister reported 1,29,000 hectares of standing crop submerged in Villupuram, Kallakurichi and Tiruvannamalai. A statewide figure of 2,11,139 hectares of agricultural and horticultural land inundated was also reported. The state declared a Calamity of Severe Nature across 14 districts and sought Rs 2,000 crore interim relief, later over Rs 6,000 crore. Compensation was Rs 17,000 per hectare for irrigated crops with over 33 percent damage.
- **Cyclone Michaung, December 2023, Andhra Pradesh.** An expert estimated 4,04,685 hectares of standing paddy affected across eight districts. Krishna district alone lost paddy on 17,000 hectares. Tenant farmers with no land title could not claim relief.
- **Cauvery delta, repeated.** Cyclone Gaja in 2018 damaged at least 1,00,000 hectares of paddy in Thanjavur alone. The 2025 northeast monsoon and Cyclone Ditwah affected about 90,000 hectares of samba and thalady paddy. The state's relief was Rs 20,000 per hectare of harvest-ready paddy, and the Chief Minister had to ask the Prime Minister to relax procurement moisture norms because rain-soaked grain was being rejected.
- **Crop insurance does not fill the gap.** Government replies in Parliament attribute PMFBY delays in Tamil Nadu to late yield data, late state premium share, and yield disputes between insurers and states. A Nagapattinam MP told the Lok Sabha in 2022 that of 10 lakh registered farmers only about 8 lakh received claims after around 10 lakh acres were inundated. Claims depend on crop-cutting experiments done after the event, so farmers have no evidence of the rain that hit their specific field.
- **The decision gap.** Farmers decide irrigation, spraying, harvest and drying on the basis of a district forecast. A ripening samba crop lodges in two days of standing water. A crop pumped the day before heavy rain wastes water and worsens waterlogging. No public service tells a farmer whether their field, at their crop stage, is at risk this week.

**Slide line:** Fengal put 1.29 lakh hectares of standing crop under water in three districts. Compensation was Rs 17,000 a hectare, paid months later, with no record of the rain that fell on that field.

## 6. Households and the vulnerable

- In the 2015 Chennai flood, 23.25 lakh homes were submerged and power and telecom were cut across the city.
- After Michaung, residents reported submerged vehicles, power cuts not restored for days, patchy phone connectivity and rationed milk.
- In southern Tamil Nadu in December 2023, 70 of 101 combined water supply schemes in Tirunelveli were knocked out, and about 250 villages had no power for five days.
- The Mumbai mortality study found rainfall deaths are concentrated in low-elevation areas, during high tides, and over 80 percent among slum residents. The people with the least ability to move are the ones with the least location-specific information.
- Warnings are issued per district. A resident of a hilltop ward and a resident of a reclaimed marsh receive the same alert, so people learn to ignore it.

**Slide line:** Twenty-three lakh homes went under in 2015. The warning they received was the same one sent to households on high ground.

## 7. Government and responders

- The Rajya Sabha reply on urban flooding attributes it to short-duration high-intensity rain compounded by unplanned growth, encroachment on water bodies and poor drainage. All three are knowable in advance for any given point, and none appear in a weather alert.
- The 2015 audit found no scientific real-time flood forecasting at the reservoir, no monitoring of releases, and a disaster authority that had never met. Relief was reactive because there was no map of where water would pond before it did.
- After the December 2023 floods, the Union Earth Sciences Minister called for more observatories to improve extreme-rain forecasting, acknowledging the observation gap.
- The Centre and the state publicly disagreed over whether the warning was adequate and whether the event qualified as a severe calamity. The dispute itself shows there is no shared, auditable record linking the forecast issued, the impact predicted, and the damage that followed.

**Slide line:** After every flood the state and the Centre argue about whether the warning was good enough. Nobody can settle it, because no system records what was forecast for each place against what actually happened there.

## 8. How to use this in the problem statement

Open with one event, not a statistic. The Kayalpattinam forecast of 20 cm against 95 cm actual, or the Chembarambakkam release with no warning, both land in one sentence.

Then the pattern across groups, one line each:
- Households: same alert for the hilltop and the marsh.
- Commuters: underpass deaths and a Rs 225 crore afternoon.
- Farmers: 1.29 lakh hectares under water, compensation months later with no field-level record.
- Operators: a reservoir with no forecasting and rules 23 years old.
- Government: a drain network built for 2 to 5 cm an hour in a city that gets 25 in a day.

Close with the gap RIIS fills: rainfall is forecast at district scale, but damage happens at street scale, and no public system converts the one into the other with reasons attached.

Numbers to avoid overstating: death tolls differ by source for every event above. Quote the official figure where one exists, and say "over" rather than an exact number otherwise.
